# Synthesis — Expectation Fidelity Scenario

## Summary

The expectation signal makes the desired product explicit before the Brief is drafted. The First Useful Step is useful only as sequencing; it cannot replace the promise.

## Expectation Fit

- The proposed workflow keeps the user promise visible through `context.md` and carries it into the approved Brief.
- The passable-but-wrong version is named and rejected before Brief approval.
- M6 uses additive-only repair when planning omits Brief coverage.

## User-Language Anchor

The expected experience is not only "the right checks ran." The user should see their desired product preserved: what the workflow is trying to give them, why it would feel worth using, what can arrive first, what remains, what would be technically done but wrong, and which trade-offs they actually accepted.
